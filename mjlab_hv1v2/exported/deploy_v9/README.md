## Control loop

```
control_dt = 0.020 s   (50 Hz)     sim_timestep 0.005, decimation 4
root_body  = pelvis
```

Each control step:

1. assemble the 498-wide observation (below);
2. `raw = policy(obs)` → 31 values;
3. `target[i] = anchor[i] + raw[i] * action_scale[i]`;
4. write `target` to the joint PD controllers, in the joint order in `deploy.json`;
5. keep `raw` — it is the `actions` observation term next step.

## Observation — 498, term-major, oldest first

| term | dim | history | contributes | source on robot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `base_ang_vel` | 3 | 5 | 15 | IMU angular velocity, **pelvis frame**, rad/s |
| `projected_gravity` | 3 | 5 | 15 | `R_pelvis^T @ [0,0,-1]` — a **unit** vector, not 9.81 |
| `joint_pos` | 31 | 5 | 155 | `q - anchor` |
| `joint_vel` | 31 | 5 | 155 | `qdot` |
| `actions` | 31 | 5 | 155 | previous **raw** output, before `action_scale` |
| `command` | 3 | 1 | 3 | operator `[vx, vy, wz]` |

**Layout — wrong ordering does not error, it silently feeds garbage.** Each
term's whole history is contiguous, *then* terms concatenate in the order
above. Oldest frame first, so the **last** `dim` values of each slice are the
current reading:

```
[ang_vel t-4..t0][gravity t-4..t0][joint_pos t-4..t0][joint_vel t-4..t0][actions t-4..t0][command t0]
```

This is **term-major**. Many stacks are frame-major — if yours is, reindex.

**At t=0, backfill all 5 slots with the first reading.** Do not zero-fill; the
policy never saw zeros during training and the first 5 steps would be
off-distribution.

## READY pose

Servo here with joint PD, then hand over to the policy. It's the same as the
anchor. That's safe because the pose was solved with the CoM over the sole centre
(**0.01 mm** off, feet flat, stance 0.24304 m, pelvis 0.97939 m).

> **It doesn't stand open-loop.** Holding it with PD and no policy, the robot
> topples within a few seconds (2.7 s in `plant_report.py` on the corrected
> model). The policy must be running. Don't use READY as a resting state.

The legs are now nearly symmetric (knees 0.350 / 0.350). The small remaining
left/right differences in hip yaw and roll are real: the hip-pitch axes are
canted about 30° and the legs aren't mirror images.

## What changed from v5

**The plant.** v9 is the first policy trained on the corrected model.
`mjcf/urdf2mjcf.py` moves the left knee axis onto its bore (the SolidWorks
export had it 11.9 mm off) and drops a 0.036° tilt on the right sole. The real
robot never had this error, so v9's simulation is the closer match to the
hardware.

**The anchor, and so the READY pose.** The home pose was re-solved for the
corrected knee. **v9's anchor is not v5's**: a runtime must take
`anchor.default_joint_pos` from this bundle. Reusing v5's anchor with this
`policy.onnx` offsets every leg action by up to 0.026 rad.

## Hardware ankle-pitch offset

v9's anchor carries **no** ankle bias, the same as v4–v8. On tahiti-d1-002,
v4–v7 needed a hardware ankle-pitch offset to stop walking backwards. The knee
fix only changes the simulation (the real knee was always on its bore), so expect
v9 to need it too. Start at the value currently in the live
`config/units/tahiti-d1-002/calibration.yaml`. HV1V1-style anchors that bake the bias
in are a different case; this one doesn't.

## Four-bar joints — READ BEFORE WRITING GAINS

Six joints are parallel four-bars driven by **two** X8-120s each:

```
left/right_ankle_pitch, left/right_ankle_roll, waist_roll, waist_pitch
```

The linkage **amplifies** commanded joint gain, so writing the simulation
`kp = 400` straight to the motors will over-stiffen them. Measure the
amplification on HV1V2 and divide first.

H1-V1 measured `ankle_pitch 2.41x, ankle_roll 1.23x, waist_roll 4.00x,
waist_pitch 4.81x` — **on H1-V1 hardware, not this robot**. Use those as a
starting point for a measurement, not as values.

`waist_yaw` is a passthrough and needs no correction.

## Pinned joints

11 of 31 have `action_scale = 0.0`: the three waist, six wrist, two head. The
policy cannot move them, so they hold the anchor. Their raw outputs are
unpriced and drift freely — **if you ever un-pin one, add a cost in the same
change or it will slam**.

Live: 12 legs, 6 shoulders, 2 elbows.

## Known limits

- **Foot clearance is still the main risk.** About 10 mm at 1.0 m/s, which is better
  than v5's 6–8 mm. But it's only **1.4–2.9 mm at slow walking, sideways stepping and
  turning**, against an 80 mm training target. Test on a clean, flat floor first, at
  low commands, with a gantry.
- **Top speed is a little lower than v5's:** about 0.86 m/s achieved at a 1.0 command
  (v5: 0.92 on the same model).
- **Cadence:** about 3.4 steps/s at 1.0 m/s, slightly calmer than v5's 3.6–3.8. Check
  that leg tracking keeps up on hardware before going above about 0.6 m/s.
- **Torque:** the worst actuator averages about 133% of its continuous rating over
  training episodes (v5: 129%), with 3.9% of actuator-steps above continuous (v5: 3.6%).
  That's inside the vendor's 10 s stall rating, but log it. Standing peak torque in
  sim2sim is 114 N.m (v5: 102). `effort_peak_nm` in `deploy.json` is the vendor
  PEAK (a 3–10 s rating); `effort_continuous_nm` is what the motor actually sustains.
- **`armature` is provisional.** It's the vendor `J·ratio²`, which assumes the
  published inertia is motor-side, and the vendor never says. It was trained with
  domain randomisation over `(0.6, 1.4)` to hedge.
- **No thermal model exists.** Watch `effort_continuous_nm`.
- **The model is the corrected knee.** Evaluating v9 on an older model, or v1–v8 on
  this one, mixes plants. `HOME_KEYFRAME_V1_V8` in `hv1v2_constants.py` is the
  old pose, for older checkpoints only.
